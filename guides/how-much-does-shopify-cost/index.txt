<h2 id="what-the-plan-price-doesnt-include">What the Plan Price Doesn't Include</h2>
<p>Budget for these on top:</p>
<p><strong>Card processing on every sale.</strong> Separate from the third-party fee above, and charged regardless of which plan you're on. Rates vary by plan tier and by country, so check Shopify's pricing page for your region rather than trusting a number from any article — including this one.</p>
<p><strong>Apps.</strong> The single most underestimated line. Reviews, email, subscriptions, upsells, and inventory tools are mostly paid monthly, and a modest stack of five apps at $15–30 each can quietly exceed your plan cost.</p>
<p><strong>Your domain.</strong> Small, but real.</p>
<p><strong>POS Pro</strong>, if you sell in person — <strong>$89/month per location</strong>.</p>
<p><strong>Themes.</strong> Free themes are genuinely usable; paid ones are a one-off cost, not recurring.</p>
<h2 id="choosing-a-tier-honestly">Choosing a Tier Honestly</h2>
<p><strong>Basic</strong> suits almost every new store. The higher tiers mainly buy lower transaction fees and more advanced reporting, neither of which matters at low volume.</p>
<p><strong>The upgrade maths is arithmetic, not ambition.</strong> Moving from Basic to Grow costs $40/month more and cuts the third-party fee from 2% to 1%. If you're on a third-party provider, that upgrade pays for itself at around <strong>$4,000/month</strong> in sales. If you use Shopify Payments, the transaction-fee saving is worth nothing to you and you should upgrade only for the features.</p>
<div class="not-prose callout callout-tip">
<p class="callout-label">💡 Pro Tip</p>
<div class="callout-body">
<p>Before upgrading, check which of the two things you're actually buying: lower fees or better features. Store owners routinely upgrade for the fee reduction while on Shopify Payments, where that reduction does not apply to them at all.</p>
</div>
</div>
<h2 id="is-it-worth-it-against-the-alternatives">Is It Worth It Against the Alternatives?</h2>
<p>Depends entirely on what you're selling.</p>
<p><strong>For physical products at volume</strong>, Shopify's per-order costs are competitive and the platform does a great deal for you.</p>
<p><strong>For digital downloads</strong>, the monthly subscription is hard to justify at low volume. Marketplace and creator platforms take a percentage but charge nothing monthly, so they win until your sales are large enough that a percentage exceeds a flat fee.</p>
<p><strong>For books specifically</strong>, a storefront competes with Amazon KDP's distribution rather than its economics — a different calculation, covered in our KDP royalties guide.</p>
<p>The general rule: <strong>flat monthly fees beat percentages once volume is high; percentages beat flat fees while you're small.</strong> Work out your own crossover point rather than following anyone's recommendation.</p>
<div class="not-prose callout callout-action">
<p class="callout-label">✅ Action Step</p>
<div class="callout-body">
<p>Before subscribing, add up all of it for your expected volume: plan + card processing + third-party fee if applicable + apps. Compare that against the same sales on a marketplace. Do it at your realistic first-year volume, not the volume you hope for — that's the number that decides whether the store survives long enough to reach the optimistic one.</p>
</div>
