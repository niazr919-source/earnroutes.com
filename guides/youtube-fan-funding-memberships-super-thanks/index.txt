YouTube's own documentation shows that ads are the <em>worse</em> deal for creators, by a wide margin.</p>
<h2 id="the-two-splits">The Two Splits</h2>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Revenue source</th>
<th>Creator receives</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>Channel memberships, Super Chat, Super Stickers, Super Thanks</td>
<td><strong>70%</strong> of net revenues</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Ads on public videos (watch page)</td>
<td><strong>55%</strong> of net revenues</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>YouTube's wording: it "will pay them 70% of net revenues from channel memberships, Super Chat, Super Stickers, and Super Thanks," and "55% of net revenues from ads displayed or streamed on their public videos."</p>
<p>That's a <strong>15 percentage point</strong> difference. For every $100 of net revenue, fan funding puts $70 in your pocket where advertising puts $55.</p>
<div class="not-prose callout callout-info">
<p class="callout-label">ℹ️ Good to Know</p>
<div class="callout-body">
<p>Put another way: YouTube keeps 45% of ad money and 30% of fan funding. A dollar given to you directly by a viewer is worth about 27% more than a dollar an advertiser spent against your video.</p>
</div>
</div>
<h2 id="why-that-doesnt-automatically-mean-fan-funding-earns-more">Why That Doesn't Automatically Mean Fan Funding Earns More</h2>
<p>The split is only half the equation. The other half is how many people pay.</p>
<p><strong>Ads earn on every view.</strong> A viewer who watches passively, never comments and never subscribes still generates ad revenue. The rate per view is small, but the base is everyone.</p>
<p><strong>Fan funding earns from a tiny minority.</strong> Only viewers who choose to join a membership or send a Super Thanks contribute anything. Most channels convert a very small percentage of their audience.</p>
<p>So the honest framing is: fan funding has a <strong>better rate on a much smaller base</strong>. Which wins depends entirely on your audience's willingness to pay — and that varies enormously by niche.</p>
<div class="not-prose callout callout-tip">
<p class="callout-label">💡 Pro Tip</p>
<div class="callout-body">
<p>The channels where fan funding outperforms ads tend to share one trait: a strong parasocial connection where viewers feel they know the creator. Tutorial and reference channels — where viewers arrive from search, get an answer and leave — usually earn far more from ads, because their audience is large but not attached.</p>
</div>
</div>
<h2 id="taxes-are-handled-separately">Taxes Are Handled Separately</h2>
<p>A useful clarification, because people assume the percentage is taken after everything.</p>
<p>YouTube states that transaction taxes such as sales tax, VAT and GST "are not revenue to Google and are not included in the partner revenue share calculation."</p>
<p>So sales tax isn't quietly reducing your 70%. It's handled outside the split entirely. Your own income tax, of course, still applies to what you receive.</p>
<h2 id="eligibility">Eligibility</h2>
<p>Fan funding features sit inside the <strong>YouTube Partner Program</strong>, so you need to be accepted into YPP first — which means meeting the subscriber and watch-hour or Shorts-views thresholds, and complying with the channel monetization policies.</p>
<p>Beyond that, YouTube requires that you live in an available location and that you (and your MCN, if you have one) have accepted and are complying with the relevant terms, including the Commerce Product Module.</p>
<div class="not-prose callout callout-warning">
<p class="callout-label">⚠️ Warning</p>
<div class="callout-body">
<p>If you're part of a multi-channel network, check your contract. YouTube notes that some MCNs take an additional revenue share, which means your actual membership income can be lower than 70%. The 70% is what YouTube pays out — not necessarily what reaches you.</p>
</div>
</div>
<h2 id="what-this-changes-in-practice">What This Changes in Practice</h2>
<p><strong>Turn fan funding on as soon as you're eligible.</strong> Memberships and Super Thanks cost nothing to enable and pay a better rate than the ads you already run. There is no downside to having them available.</p>
<p><strong>Don't expect them to replace ads at small scale.</strong> With a modest audience, a handful of members will not outweigh ad revenue from all your views. Treat fan funding as an additional layer, not a substitute.</p>
<p><strong>Recognise which business you're in.</strong> If your channel is search-driven and impersonal, ads are your engine and you should optimise watch time. If your audience follows <em>you</em>, the 70% split rewards building that relationship directly.</p>
<p><strong>Remember Shorts are different again.</strong> Shorts revenue goes through a pooled model with its own fixed 45% creator share, which is lower than both figures here.</p>
<div class="not-prose callout callout-action">
<p class="callout-label">✅ Action Step</p>
<div class="callout-body">
<p>Check whether memberships and Super Thanks are switched on for your channel right now. Many creators who qualify never enable them, leaving the better-paying revenue stream turned off while optimising the worse-paying one.</p>
</div>
</div>
