<h2 id="apple-15-under-1-million">Apple: 15% Under $1 Million</h2>
<p>Apple's <strong>App Store Small Business Program</strong> charges <strong>15%</strong> on paid apps and In-App Purchases, against the standard <strong>30%</strong>.</p>
<p>To qualify you need up to <strong>$1 million USD in proceeds in the prior calendar year</strong>. That covers the overwhelming majority of independent developers — $1 million in <em>proceeds</em>, not revenue, is a substantial business.</p>
<p>What happens when you outgrow it is worth knowing precisely. Apple states:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>"If a participating developer surpasses the 1 million USD threshold in the current calendar year, the standard commission rate will apply to future sales."</p>
</blockquote>
<p>So the change applies going forward, not retroactively — you don't get billed the difference on earlier sales. And if proceeds fall back below the threshold, you can qualify again the following year.</p>
<div class="not-prose callout callout-info">
<p class="callout-label">ℹ️ Good to Know</p>
<div class="callout-body">
<p>Note that it's the <strong>prior</strong> calendar year that decides your eligibility, but the <strong>current</strong> year that can knock you out mid-stream. A developer whose app takes off in March can spend the rest of that year at 30% before requalifying is even a question.</p>
</div>
</div>
<p>Apple also lists a further reduction for developers on alternative terms in the EU: <strong>10% on subscriptions after their first year</strong>.</p>
<h2 id="google-play-it-depends-where-your-users-are">Google Play: It Depends Where Your Users Are</h2>
<p>Google Play now runs two different structures depending on the market.</p>
<p><strong>In markets not yet on the new model</strong>, the familiar arrangement applies:</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Earnings</th>
<th>Service fee</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>First $1 million USD annually</td>
<td><strong>15%</strong></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Above $1 million</td>
<td><strong>30%</strong></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Auto-renewing subscriptions</td>
<td><strong>15%</strong> regardless of total revenue</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>That subscription rate is the quiet advantage. A subscription app pays 15% at any scale, while a paid-app or IAP business crosses into 30% once it passes $1 million.</p>
<h2 id="the-30-june-2026-restructure">The 30 June 2026 Restructure</h2>
<p><strong>In the EEA, UK and US</strong>, Google Play moved to a materially different model from <strong>30 June 2026</strong> — and this is where most published advice is now wrong.</p>
<p>Two changes at once. The charge splits into a <strong>service fee plus a 5% billing fee</strong>, and the service fee depends on <strong>when the user first installed or updated your app</strong>.</p>
<p>Google defines a <em>new install</em> as "a transaction from a user whose first-time install or first update of the app from Google Play occurred on or after June 30, 2026", and an <em>existing install</em> as one where that happened before.</p>
<p>Roughly, for standard transactions:</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>User type</th>
<th>Standard transaction</th>
<th>Auto-renewing subscriptions</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td><strong>New install</strong></td>
<td>10% + 5% billing fee</td>
<td>10% + 5% billing fee</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>Existing install</strong></td>
<td>25% + 5% billing fee</td>
<td>10% + 5% billing fee</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>Lower rates apply for developers participating in Google's Play Games Level Up or Apps Experience programs, and separate rates apply to transactions through external web links.</p>
<div class="not-prose callout callout-warning">
<p class="callout-label">⚠️ Warning</p>
<div class="callout-body">
<p>This is genuinely complicated, and the table above is a simplification of a schedule with several branches. Google's own page is the authority — read it against your specific app, market and program participation rather than budgeting from any summary, including this one.</p>
</div>
</div>
<p>The headline effect: a <em>new</em> user acquired after 30 June 2026 costs you far less in fees than a long-standing one on a standard transaction, while subscriptions land at 15% all-in either way.</p>
<h2 id="what-this-means-before-you-build">What This Means Before You Build</h2>
<p><strong>Subscriptions are treated favourably on both stores.</strong> Google Play charges 15% for auto-renewing subscriptions regardless of revenue, and Apple's EU alternative terms drop to 10% after year one. If your idea works as a subscription, the fee structure rewards that.</p>
<p><strong>The $1 million threshold is further away than it feels.</strong> Most independent developers will never approach it, which means the realistic rate to plan around is 15%, not 30%. Revenue models built on a 30% assumption understate what you'd actually keep.</p>
<p><strong>Fees are not your only cost.</strong> Both platforms charge developer program membership annually, and app income is self-employment income — in the US, self-employment tax alone is 15.3% of net earnings before income tax.</p>
<div class="not-prose callout callout-tip">
<p class="callout-label">💡 Pro Tip</p>
<div class="callout-body">
<p>Compare this against other digital-product routes before committing. An app keeping 85% of revenue sounds worse than a course platform paying 97% on your own referrals — until you account for the fact that app stores put your product in front of an audience actively searching, which is exactly what the higher-paying routes make you supply yourself.</p>
</div>
</div>
<h2 id="a-practical-check">A Practical Check</h2>
<p>Work out three numbers for your own situation:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Which store rate applies to you</strong> — almost certainly 15%, not 30%</li>
<li><strong>Whether your market is on Google Play's new structure</strong> (EEA, UK, US) or the older one</li>
<li><strong>Whether a subscription model fits</strong>, given both stores price subscriptions more favourably</li>
</ol>
<div class="not-prose callout callout-action">
<p class="callout-label">✅ Action Step</p>
<div class="callout-body">
<p>If you already ship an app in the EEA, UK or US, check how your revenue splits between new and existing installs since 30 June 2026. That split now directly changes your effective fee rate, and it is not something the old "30% to Apple and Google" model captures at all.</p>
</div>
