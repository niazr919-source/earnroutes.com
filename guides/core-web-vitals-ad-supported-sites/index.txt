For an ad-supported site they matter twice over: they are a ranking consideration, and the thing damaging them is the thing paying your bills.</p>
<h2 id="the-three-numbers">The Three Numbers</h2>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Metric</th>
<th>What it measures</th>
<th>Google's "good" threshold</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td><strong>LCP</strong> — Largest Contentful Paint</td>
<td>How quickly the main content appears</td>
<td>Within <strong>2.5 seconds</strong></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>INP</strong> — Interaction to Next Paint</td>
<td>How quickly the page responds to input</td>
<td><strong>200 ms</strong> or less</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>CLS</strong> — Cumulative Layout Shift</td>
<td>How much the layout jumps around</td>
<td><strong>0.1</strong> or less</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>Two details that change how you read your own reports:</p>
<p><strong>Assessment uses the 75th percentile</strong> of page loads, segmented across mobile and desktop. So you are not judged on your average visitor — you are judged on a slow-ish one. A site that feels fine on your laptop can fail comfortably.</p>
<p><strong>INP replaced FID.</strong> Interaction to Next Paint became a stable Core Web Vital in 2024, superseding First Input Delay, because it measures runtime responsiveness more comprehensively. Any guide still telling you to optimise FID is out of date.</p>
<div class="not-prose callout callout-warning">
<p class="callout-label">⚠️ Warning</p>
<div class="callout-body">
<p>Check whether your performance advice predates 2024. FID measured only the delay before the <em>first</em> interaction began processing, which was easy to score well on. INP measures the full response across the whole visit, and many sites that passed FID comfortably fail INP.</p>
</div>
</div>
<h2 id="why-ads-attack-all-three">Why Ads Attack All Three</h2>
<p>This is the part generic performance guides skip, because they assume a site with no ads.</p>
<p><strong>CLS is the obvious one.</strong> An ad unit that loads without reserved space pushes everything below it down the page. That downward shove is precisely what CLS measures, and ad slots are the most common cause of a failing score on content sites.</p>
<p><strong>LCP suffers</strong> because ad scripts compete for bandwidth and the main thread during the exact window when your largest element — usually the hero image or heading — is trying to paint.</p>
<p><strong>INP suffers</strong> because ad libraries run JavaScript continuously: bidding, refreshing, measuring viewability. Every one of those tasks occupies the main thread and delays the response to a tap.</p>
<p>So the honest framing is a trade, not a free optimisation. More ad units mean more revenue per pageview and worse vitals.</p>
<h2 id="the-fixes-that-actually-work">The Fixes That Actually Work</h2>
<p><strong>Reserve space for every ad slot.</strong> The single highest-value change. Give each container an explicit minimum height matching the ad size you expect, so the space exists before the ad arrives and nothing moves when it does. If your slots are already styled with fixed dimensions, most of your CLS problem is solved.</p>
<p><strong>Load ad scripts asynchronously</strong>, never blocking render. AdSense's own tag is async by default — the damage usually comes from additional tags added later.</p>
<p><strong>Don't put an ad above your LCP element.</strong> A banner competing with your hero for the first paint directly worsens LCP. Below-the-fold placements cost less performance for a similar viewability outcome.</p>
<p><strong>Be careful about collapsing unfilled units.</strong> AdSense lets you hide unfilled slots with CSS targeting <code>data-ad-status="unfilled"</code>. It removes the empty gap, but it also removes the reserved space <em>after</em> the page rendered — which shifts content and raises CLS. If your unfilled rate is high enough to make this tempting, reducing the number of ad units is usually the better fix.</p>
<p><strong>Cut ad density before you cut anything else.</strong> Six units on a 1,200-word article is both a performance problem and a poor experience. Fewer, better-placed units frequently earn more per session because readers stay.</p>
<div class="not-prose callout callout-tip">
<p class="callout-label">💡 Pro Tip</p>
<div class="callout-body">
<p>There's a useful convergence here: AdSense's placement policy already tells you to keep ads away from links, buttons and navigation to avoid accidental clicks. Generous spacing around ad slots satisfies that policy <em>and</em> gives you room to reserve fixed dimensions. The compliant layout and the fast layout are largely the same layout.</p>
</div>
</div>
<h2 id="how-to-measure-honestly">How to Measure Honestly</h2>
<p><strong>Search Console's Core Web Vitals report</strong> uses field data — real visits from real users — and it's what actually counts. It's also slow to update, because it works on a rolling 28-day window.</p>
<p><strong>PageSpeed Insights</strong> gives you both: field data if Google has enough, plus a lab test you can run instantly. Use the lab score to iterate on a fix and the field data to confirm it worked.</p>
<div class="not-prose callout callout-warning">
<p class="callout-label">⚠️ Warning</p>
<div class="callout-body">
<p>Lab tests often understate the ad problem. Testing tools may not load the same ad inventory a real visitor gets, so your lab CLS can look healthy while field CLS fails. When the two disagree on an ad-supported site, trust the field data.</p>
</div>
</div>
<h2 id="a-realistic-target">A Realistic Target</h2>
<p>Chasing a perfect score on an ad-supported site is not a good use of your time. Google's thresholds are pass/fail bands, not a leaderboard — a page comfortably inside "good" gains nothing further from being faster.</p>
<p>A sensible order of work:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Reserve space for every ad slot.</strong> Usually fixes CLS outright.</li>
<li><strong>Move ads out of the LCP region.</strong></li>
<li><strong>Audit total ad count per page.</strong> Remove the weakest performers.</li>
<li><strong>Measure in Search Console field data</strong>, not just lab tests.</li>
<li><strong>Stop when you're inside the thresholds.</strong></li>
</ol>
<div class="not-prose callout callout-action">
<p class="callout-label">✅ Action Step</p>
<div class="callout-body">
<p>Open PageSpeed Insights on your busiest article and look only at CLS first. If it fails, check whether every ad container has an explicit height in your CSS. On most ad-supported sites that one change moves CLS from failing to passing without touching anything else.</p>
</div>
