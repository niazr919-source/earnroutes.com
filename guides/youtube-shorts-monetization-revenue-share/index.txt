Understanding the actual mechanism explains why two channels with identical view counts can earn very different amounts.</p>
<p>YouTube describes it as four steps.</p>
<h2 id="step-1-pool-the-revenue">Step 1: Pool the Revenue</h2>
<p>Ad revenue from ads shown between Shorts in the Shorts Feed goes into a monthly pool. It is not attributed to individual videos the way long-form pre-roll is.</p>
<p>This is the fundamental difference from long-form. On a standard video, ads run against <em>your</em> video and the revenue is traceable to it. In the Shorts Feed, ads run between many creators' Shorts as the viewer scrolls, so there's nothing to attribute.</p>
<h2 id="step-2-calculate-the-creator-pool-where-music-bites">Step 2: Calculate the Creator Pool — Where Music Bites</h2>
<p>From that pooled revenue, YouTube calculates the <strong>Creator Pool</strong> based on engaged views and music usage.</p>
<p>This is the step that surprises people:</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Music in your Short</th>
<th>Share of that revenue going to the Creator Pool</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>No music</td>
<td>Full amount</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>1 track</strong></td>
<td><strong>Half</strong> — the other half covers music licensing</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>2 tracks</strong></td>
<td><strong>One third</strong> — the other two thirds cover licensing</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>YouTube's wording for the two-track case: "one third of the revenue associated with its engaged views would be allocated to the Creator Pool, and the other two thirds used to cover the costs of music licensing."</p>
<div class="not-prose callout callout-warning">
<p class="callout-label">⚠️ Warning</p>
<div class="callout-body">
<p>Adding a second music track to a Short cuts the revenue reaching the Creator Pool from that Short by two thirds. Music is not free just because YouTube's library lets you use it — the licence is paid for out of the money that would otherwise have been creator revenue.</p>
</div>
</div>
<h2 id="step-3-allocate-by-engaged-views">Step 3: Allocate by Engaged Views</h2>
<p>The Creator Pool is then divided according to each creator's share of total engaged views.</p>
<p>One important protection here: YouTube states creators receive allocation based on "100% of the total number of engaged views on their Shorts, regardless of whether any music... is used."</p>
<p>So music reduces the size of the pool, but it does not shrink <em>your slice</em> of it. You are not individually penalised for using music — the whole pool is smaller, and everyone draws from the smaller pool.</p>
<div class="not-prose callout callout-info">
<p class="callout-label">ℹ️ Good to Know</p>
<div class="callout-body">
<p>That distinction matters when reading advice on this. "Using music reduces your earnings" is true at the ecosystem level and false at the individual-allocation level. Your slice is calculated on all your engaged views either way.</p>
</div>
</div>
<h2 id="step-4-apply-the-45-revenue-share">Step 4: Apply the 45% Revenue Share</h2>
<p>Finally, creators keep <strong>"45% of their allocated revenue, regardless if music was used or not."</strong></p>
<p>That rate is fixed. It does not improve with subscriber count, channel age, or Partner Program tier.</p>
<h2 id="why-shorts-rpm-looks-so-low">Why Shorts RPM Looks So Low</h2>
<p>Put the steps together and the arithmetic explains itself. Revenue is pooled across an enormous volume of views, reduced by music licensing before allocation, split by share of engaged views, then multiplied by 0.45.</p>
<p>Compare that with long-form, where ads attach to your specific video and the creator share is considerably higher. This is why creators consistently report that a million Shorts views earns a fraction of what a million long-form views does.</p>
<p>Shorts are a discovery and audience-building surface. Treating them as a primary revenue stream at small scale sets you up for disappointment.</p>
<h2 id="you-must-opt-in-and-it-isnt-backdated">You Must Opt In, and It Isn't Backdated</h2>
<p>Only creators who have <strong>accepted the Shorts Monetization Module</strong> earn Shorts revenue, and YouTube is explicit that <strong>prior views don't qualify</strong>.</p>
<p>If you joined the Partner Program but never accepted the Shorts module, Shorts you have already published have earned nothing and accepting now will not change that retroactively. Check your monetization settings.</p>
<div class="not-prose callout callout-action">
<p class="callout-label">✅ Action Step</p>
<div class="callout-body">
<p>Two things to check today: that you have actually accepted the Shorts Monetization Module, and how many music tracks your typical Short uses. If you routinely layer two tracks, dropping to one doubles the share of that revenue reaching the Creator Pool.</p>
</div>
</div>
<h2 id="the-policies-still-apply">The Policies Still Apply</h2>
<p>Shorts revenue sits inside the Partner Program, so the channel monetization policies apply in full — including the requirement that content be "original and authentic" and not "mass-produced, generic, repetitive, or manipulative."</p>
<p>That is worth stating plainly because Shorts are the format most vulnerable to templated, high-volume production. The economics reward volume; the policies punish sameness. Reconciling those two pressures is the actual difficulty of building a Shorts channel.</p>
