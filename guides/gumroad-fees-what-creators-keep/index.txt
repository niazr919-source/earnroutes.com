<p>Gumroad describes the second as applying "when new customers find and buy from you through our discover marketplace."</p>
<p>There is no monthly subscription. Gumroad states its pricing has "no hidden fees, no monthly charges" — you pay only when you sell.</p>
<div class="not-prose callout callout-info">
<p class="callout-label">ℹ️ Good to Know</p>
<div class="callout-body">
<p>That structure tells you what Gumroad thinks it's selling. At 10% it is a checkout and delivery system. At 30% it is a storefront doing your marketing. Both are defensible prices for genuinely different services.</p>

<h2 id="the-part-most-comparisons-miss">The Part Most Comparisons Miss</h2>
<p>Since <strong>1 January 2025</strong>, Gumroad acts as <strong>Merchant of Record</strong>, and states it "handles ALL your tax obligations" including managing "sales tax collection and remittance worldwide."</p>
<p>This is easy to skim past and genuinely valuable. Selling digital products internationally means facing EU VAT, UK VAT, and a patchwork of US state sales tax rules — obligations that can apply from your first sale, with no minimum threshold in some jurisdictions.</p>
<p>As Merchant of Record, Gumroad is the seller of record for tax purposes and takes that on.</p>
<div class="not-prose callout callout-warning">
<p class="callout-label">⚠️ Warning</p>
<div class="callout-body">
<p>This changes fee comparisons materially. A platform charging 5% where you handle your own global VAT registration and filing is not obviously cheaper than one charging 10% that handles it for you. Compliance has a real cost, whether you pay it in accountant fees or your own time — and getting it wrong has a cost too.</p>
<p>Note this covers <em>sales</em> tax on transactions. Your own income tax on the money you receive is still yours to deal with.</p>
</div>
</div>

<p>Udemy's spread is far more extreme, but the principle is identical — and so is the strategic conclusion. <strong>The audience you own is the asset.</strong> Every platform in this category prices access to <em>its</em> audience at a steep premium, because that's the expensive thing to build.</p>
<p>Gumroad's version is comparatively gentle. Even at its worst rate you keep 70%, where Udemy's marketplace rate leaves you 37%.</p>
<h2 id="when-gumroad-makes-sense">When Gumroad Makes Sense</h2>
<p><strong>It fits well</strong> if you already have somewhere to send people — a newsletter, a following, a site — and want checkout, file delivery and tax compliance without building any of it. At 10% + $0.50, that's cheap infrastructure.</p>
<p><strong>It fits less well</strong> as a discovery engine. Discover exists and does bring buyers, but at 30% it should be a bonus rather than your plan.</p>
<p><strong>Watch small-ticket pricing.</strong> At $5 a product you're surrendering 20% to the fixed fee alone. Bundling several small items into one higher-priced product improves your effective rate meaningfully.</p>
<div class="not-prose callout callout-action">
<p class="callout-label">✅ Action Step</p>
<div class="callout-body">
<p>Look at your last month of Gumroad sales and split them by Discover versus direct. If a large share came through Discover, the fastest available margin improvement is not raising prices — it's getting more of those same buyers to arrive through your own link.</p>
