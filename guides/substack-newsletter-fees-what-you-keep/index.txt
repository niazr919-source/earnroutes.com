It's accurate and it's incomplete, because Substack's cut is one of <strong>three</strong> separate charges on every payment.</p>
<h2 id="publishing-is-genuinely-free">Publishing Is Genuinely Free</h2>
<p>Worth stating first, because it's the genuinely good part of the deal: Substack says publishing is free "no matter how many subscribers you have."</p>
<p>There is no monthly platform fee, no per-email cost, and no subscriber tier pricing. You can build a list of fifty thousand free readers and pay nothing. Fees begin only when you enable paid subscriptions, and only on money you actually collect.</p>
<p>That is a materially better arrangement than most email platforms, which charge by list size whether you earn anything or not.</p>

<h2 id="annual-plans-quietly-pay-you-more">Annual Plans Quietly Pay You More</h2>
<p>Here's the part almost nobody calculates. Stripe's <strong>$0.30 is a fixed fee per transaction</strong>, so it lands twelve times on a monthly plan and once on an annual one.</p>

It's the highest-leverage pricing decision available to a newsletter, and it costs nothing to offer.</p>

<h2 id="what-isnt-included">What Isn't Included</h2>
<p>Two costs sit outside this table:</p>
<p><strong>Payouts and currency.</strong> Stripe pays into your bank account, and if that involves a currency conversion, there's a spread on top. The same trap that catches freelancers using PayPal applies here.</p>
<p><strong>Tax.</strong> Subscription revenue is income. In the US, self-employment tax alone is 15.3% of net earnings before income tax. Roughly 83% of gross is what reaches your business, not what reaches you.</p>
