<p>The gap is that PayPal's headline rate is only the first of three charges that can stack on the same payment. None of them are hidden — they're all on PayPal's published fee page — but they're on different lines, and nobody adds them up before quoting a rate.</p>

<h2 id="charge-one-the-commercial-transaction-rate">Charge One: The Commercial Transaction Rate</h2>
<p>For US commercial transactions on standard payment methods, PayPal charges <strong>3.49% plus a fixed fee</strong> — <strong>$0.49</strong> when receiving USD.</p>
<p>Different payment types carry different rates:</p>

<th>Rate</th>

<td>2.99%</td>

<p>On a $500 domestic invoice at the standard rate: $17.45 + $0.49 = <strong>$17.94</strong>. You keep $482.06.</p>
<div class="not-prose callout callout-info">
<p class="callout-label">ℹ️ Good to Know</p>
<div class="callout-body">
<p>The fixed fee is why small invoices hurt disproportionately. On a $20 payment, $0.49 is another 2.45% on top of the percentage — so the effective rate is nearly 6%. Batching small jobs into one monthly invoice measurably improves what you keep.</p>
</div>
</div>
<h2 id="charge-two-the-cross-border-surcharge">Charge Two: The Cross-Border Surcharge</h2>
<p>Here's where the "about 3%" mental model breaks.</p>
<p>PayPal adds <strong>1.50%</strong> for commercial transactions that cross borders. It applies on top of the domestic rate, not instead of it.</p>
<p>So a US freelancer receiving from an overseas client pays <strong>3.49% + 1.50% = 4.99%</strong>, plus the $0.49 fixed fee.</p>
<p>That same $500 invoice: $24.95 + $0.49 = <strong>$25.44</strong>. You keep $474.56.</p>
<h2 id="charge-three-the-currency-conversion-spread">Charge Three: The Currency Conversion Spread</h2>
<p>This is the one that catches people, because it isn't presented as a fee at all — it's built into the exchange rate.</p>
<p>When PayPal converts currencies, it applies a spread of up to <strong>4.00%</strong> for most transactions (<strong>3.00%</strong> for certain others). That is charged against the <em>converted amount</em>, on top of everything above.</p>
<p>If your client pays in EUR and your balance is USD, PayPal converts on receipt and takes the spread on the way through. On $500 that is up to another <strong>$20</strong>.</p>
<div class="not-prose callout callout-warning">
<p class="callout-label">⚠️ Warning</p>
<div class="callout-body">
<p>A 4% conversion spread can exceed the entire commercial transaction fee. Freelancers routinely optimise the 3.49% they can see on the receipt and ignore a larger charge buried in the exchange rate.</p>
</div>
</div>

<h2 id="what-to-actually-do-about-it">What to Actually Do About It</h2>
<p><strong>Check your own country's fee page first.</strong> The structure above holds broadly, but the numbers don't. This matters more than any optimisation.</p>
<p><strong>Avoid automatic conversion where you can.</strong> If your account supports holding a balance in the currency you're paid in, receiving in that currency and converting deliberately — through a provider that publishes a smaller spread — usually beats letting PayPal convert on receipt. Availability depends on your country and account type.</p>
<p><strong>Price the fee in.</strong> If most of your clients are overseas, roughly 5% before conversion is your real cost of collection. Quote accordingly rather than discovering it afterwards.</p>
<p><strong>Batch small invoices.</strong> The fixed fee makes frequent small payments expensive. One monthly invoice beats six weekly ones.</p>
<p><strong>Compare alternatives honestly.</strong> Payoneer and Wise both publish their pricing openly and are commonly cheaper for cross-border freelance work, particularly on conversion. Both are linked below — read their current fee pages rather than trusting any comparison table, including one on this site.</p>
<p><strong>Never use "friends and family" to dodge fees.</strong> Clients occasionally suggest it. It removes your seller protection entirely, and for a genuine commercial payment it misrepresents the transaction to PayPal. If a dispute arises, you have no recourse.</p>
<div class="not-prose callout callout-action">
<p class="callout-label">✅ Action Step</p>
<div class="callout-body">
<p>Open your last three PayPal payments and compare the amount the client sent against what landed. If the gap is bigger than the headline rate, you're paying the cross-border surcharge, the conversion spread, or both — and both are addressable.</p>
