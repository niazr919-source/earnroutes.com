What PayPal Actually Takes From a Freelance Payment (And the Fee Nobody Warns You About)

PayPal's headline rate is 3.49% + $0.49, but international freelancers pay two more charges on top. Here's the real arithmetic on a cross-border invoice, with every rate from PayPal's own fee page.

Skill level:BeginnerSetup time:20 minutes to audit your invoicesTime to first dollar:Immediate on your next paymentEarnings potential:Recovers 3–9% of every invoice

Prerequisites

  • A PayPal account used to receive client payments
  • A recent invoice you can check the deductions on

Most freelancers know PayPal takes "about 3%." Then a $500 invoice from an overseas client lands as $455 and the arithmetic doesn't work.

The gap is that PayPal's headline rate is only the first of three charges that can stack on the same payment. None of them are hidden — they're all on PayPal's published fee page — but they're on different lines, and nobody adds them up before quoting a rate.

⚠️ Warning

All figures below are from PayPal's US fee schedule. PayPal publishes different rates for every country it operates in, and they vary a lot. Use these numbers to understand the structure, then check your own region's page before pricing work.

Charge One: The Commercial Transaction Rate

For US commercial transactions on standard payment methods, PayPal charges 3.49% plus a fixed fee — $0.49 when receiving USD.

Different payment types carry different rates:

Payment type Rate
Standard commercial transaction 3.49% + $0.49
Send/receive money for goods and services 2.99%
QR code transactions 2.29% + $0.09

On a $500 domestic invoice at the standard rate: $17.45 + $0.49 = $17.94. You keep $482.06.

ℹ️ Good to Know

The fixed fee is why small invoices hurt disproportionately. On a $20 payment, $0.49 is another 2.45% on top of the percentage — so the effective rate is nearly 6%. Batching small jobs into one monthly invoice measurably improves what you keep.

Charge Two: The Cross-Border Surcharge

Here's where the "about 3%" mental model breaks.

PayPal adds 1.50% for commercial transactions that cross borders. It applies on top of the domestic rate, not instead of it.

So a US freelancer receiving from an overseas client pays 3.49% + 1.50% = 4.99%, plus the $0.49 fixed fee.

That same $500 invoice: $24.95 + $0.49 = $25.44. You keep $474.56.

Charge Three: The Currency Conversion Spread

This is the one that catches people, because it isn't presented as a fee at all — it's built into the exchange rate.

When PayPal converts currencies, it applies a spread of up to 4.00% for most transactions (3.00% for certain others). That is charged against the converted amount, on top of everything above.

If your client pays in EUR and your balance is USD, PayPal converts on receipt and takes the spread on the way through. On $500 that is up to another $20.

⚠️ Warning

A 4% conversion spread can exceed the entire commercial transaction fee. Freelancers routinely optimise the 3.49% they can see on the receipt and ignore a larger charge buried in the exchange rate.

The Full Arithmetic

A $500 invoice from an overseas client, paid in a currency you don't hold:

Charge Amount
Commercial transaction (3.49%) $17.45
Cross-border surcharge (1.50%) $7.50
Fixed fee $0.49
Currency conversion (up to 4.00%) up to $20.00
Total up to $45.44
You keep about $454.56

That's roughly 9%, not 3%. On $40,000 of annual freelance income routed this way, the difference between assuming 3% and paying 9% is about $2,400 a year.

What to Actually Do About It

Check your own country's fee page first. The structure above holds broadly, but the numbers don't. This matters more than any optimisation.

Avoid automatic conversion where you can. If your account supports holding a balance in the currency you're paid in, receiving in that currency and converting deliberately — through a provider that publishes a smaller spread — usually beats letting PayPal convert on receipt. Availability depends on your country and account type.

Price the fee in. If most of your clients are overseas, roughly 5% before conversion is your real cost of collection. Quote accordingly rather than discovering it afterwards.

Batch small invoices. The fixed fee makes frequent small payments expensive. One monthly invoice beats six weekly ones.

Compare alternatives honestly. Payoneer and Wise both publish their pricing openly and are commonly cheaper for cross-border freelance work, particularly on conversion. Both are linked below — read their current fee pages rather than trusting any comparison table, including one on this site.

Never use "friends and family" to dodge fees. Clients occasionally suggest it. It removes your seller protection entirely, and for a genuine commercial payment it misrepresents the transaction to PayPal. If a dispute arises, you have no recourse.

✅ Action Step

Open your last three PayPal payments and compare the amount the client sent against what landed. If the gap is bigger than the headline rate, you're paying the cross-border surcharge, the conversion spread, or both — and both are addressable.

Where to Verify Any of This

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does PayPal charge freelancers to receive money?

For US commercial transactions PayPal charges 3.49% plus a fixed fee of $0.49 for USD on standard payment methods. Some payment types carry different rates — 2.99% for send/receive money for goods and services, and 2.29% + $0.09 for QR code transactions.

What is PayPal's international fee for freelancers?

PayPal adds a 1.50% cross-border surcharge on top of the domestic rate for commercial transactions that cross borders. On a US commercial transaction that takes the percentage from 3.49% to 4.99%, before any currency conversion.

Why did I receive so much less than my client sent?

Three charges usually stack: the commercial transaction rate, the 1.50% cross-border surcharge if the client is abroad, and a currency conversion spread of up to 4.00% if the money arrives in a different currency than your balance. The conversion spread is the one most freelancers never account for.

Can you avoid PayPal's currency conversion fee?

Often, by holding a balance in the currency you are paid in rather than letting PayPal convert on receipt, then converting through a cheaper route. Whether this is available depends on your country and account type — check PayPal's fee page for your region before assuming.
