"Shopify costs $25 a month" is true and misleading in equal measure. The subscription is the part you see; the fees attached to every order are the part that determines whether your margin survives.

Here's the full picture, with Shopify's own published numbers.

## The Plans

| Plan | Paid monthly | Paid yearly | Third-party transaction fee |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Basic | $25/mo | $19/mo | 2% |
| Grow | $65/mo | $49/mo | 1% |
| Advanced | $399/mo | $299/mo | 0.6% |
| Plus | from $2,300/mo | — | 0.2% |

Shopify also advertises a **3-day free trial, then $1/month for 3 months**, which is genuinely useful for testing whether a store idea works before committing.

:::info
The annual discount is meaningful — Basic drops from $25 to $19, about $72 a year. But it commits you for twelve months. Take it once you know the store works, not while you're still deciding whether to keep it.
:::

## The Fee Most People Miss

Look at the right-hand column again. That third-party transaction fee is charged **on top of** card processing, on **every order**, and it applies only if you use a payment provider other than **Shopify Payments**.

On Basic that's **2% of every sale**.

Work it through on a store doing $5,000/month:

| | Using Shopify Payments | Using a third-party provider |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basic plan | $25 | $25 |
| Third-party fee (2%) | $0 | $100 |
| **Extra per month** | — | **$100** |
| **Extra per year** | — | **$1,200** |

That $1,200 is four times the annual cost of the plan itself. It is easily the most expensive decision in the Shopify pricing table, and it's presented as a footnote.

:::warning
This fee is why "which payment provider should I use" is not a minor setup detail on Shopify. If Shopify Payments is available in your country and supports your business type, using anything else needs to be a deliberate, costed decision — not a default you drifted into.
:::

## What the Plan Price Doesn't Include

Budget for these on top:

**Card processing on every sale.** Separate from the third-party fee above, and charged regardless of which plan you're on. Rates vary by plan tier and by country, so check Shopify's pricing page for your region rather than trusting a number from any article — including this one.

**Apps.** The single most underestimated line. Reviews, email, subscriptions, upsells, and inventory tools are mostly paid monthly, and a modest stack of five apps at $15–30 each can quietly exceed your plan cost.

**Your domain.** Small, but real.

**POS Pro**, if you sell in person — **$89/month per location**.

**Themes.** Free themes are genuinely usable; paid ones are a one-off cost, not recurring.

## Choosing a Tier Honestly

**Basic** suits almost every new store. The higher tiers mainly buy lower transaction fees and more advanced reporting, neither of which matters at low volume.

**The upgrade maths is arithmetic, not ambition.** Moving from Basic to Grow costs $40/month more and cuts the third-party fee from 2% to 1%. If you're on a third-party provider, that upgrade pays for itself at around **$4,000/month** in sales. If you use Shopify Payments, the transaction-fee saving is worth nothing to you and you should upgrade only for the features.

:::tip
Before upgrading, check which of the two things you're actually buying: lower fees or better features. Store owners routinely upgrade for the fee reduction while on Shopify Payments, where that reduction does not apply to them at all.
:::

## Is It Worth It Against the Alternatives?

Depends entirely on what you're selling.

**For physical products at volume**, Shopify's per-order costs are competitive and the platform does a great deal for you.

**For digital downloads**, the monthly subscription is hard to justify at low volume. Marketplace and creator platforms take a percentage but charge nothing monthly, so they win until your sales are large enough that a percentage exceeds a flat fee.

**For books specifically**, a storefront competes with Amazon KDP's distribution rather than its economics — a different calculation, covered in our KDP royalties guide.

The general rule: **flat monthly fees beat percentages once volume is high; percentages beat flat fees while you're small.** Work out your own crossover point rather than following anyone's recommendation.

:::action
Before subscribing, add up all of it for your expected volume: plan + card processing + third-party fee if applicable + apps. Compare that against the same sales on a marketplace. Do it at your realistic first-year volume, not the volume you hope for — that's the number that decides whether the store survives long enough to reach the optimistic one.
:::
