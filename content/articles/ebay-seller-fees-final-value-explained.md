Most sellers learn eBay's fees as a single number — "about 13%" — and price everything against it. eBay's own schedule shows the rate depends heavily on **what you list it under**, and the spread is wide enough to decide whether an item is worth selling at all.

## The Standard Structure

For most categories, on Store subscriptions:

| Component | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Final value fee, up to $2,500 per item | **12.7%** of the total sale |
| Portion of the sale above $2,500 | **2.35%** |
| Per-order fee, orders **$10 or less** | **$0.30** |
| Per-order fee, orders **over $10** | **$0.40** |

Note that the final value fee applies to the **total amount of the sale** — not just the item price. Shipping you charge the buyer is part of that total.

:::info
The $2,500 breakpoint is genuinely generous and rarely mentioned. On a $4,000 item you pay 12.7% on the first $2,500 and only 2.35% on the remaining $1,500 — an effective rate of about 8.8%. High-value items are proportionally cheaper to sell than mid-range ones.
:::

## Category Changes Everything

This is the part that should shape what you sell. From eBay's published schedule:

| Category | Final value fee |
| --- | --- |
| **Guitars & Basses** | **6.7%** |
| Most categories | **12.7%** |
| **Books & Magazines** | **15.3%** |

Books are charged **more than double** the rate of guitars. On a $200 sale that's $30.60 versus $13.40 — a $17 difference on identical revenue, decided entirely by which category the listing sits in.

:::warning
This is not an invitation to miscategorise listings. Listing an item in the wrong category to reduce fees breaches eBay's policies and risks the listing and the account. The point is that fee rates should inform **what you choose to sell**, not how you describe it.
:::

## The International Fee Sellers Forget

eBay charges an additional **1.65% of the total sale amount** when the buyer's registered address or delivery address is outside the US — unless you use eBay International Shipping.

That takes a standard sale from 12.7% to roughly 14.35% before the per-order fee. It's small per transaction and easy to overlook when a meaningful share of your buyers are overseas.

## What a Sale Actually Nets

A $100 sale in a standard 12.7% category, domestic:

| | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Sale total | $100.00 |
| Final value fee (12.7%) | −$12.70 |
| Per-order fee | −$0.40 |
| **You keep** | **$86.90** |

The same sale to an international buyer:

| | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Sale total | $100.00 |
| Final value fee (12.7%) | −$12.70 |
| International fee (1.65%) | −$1.65 |
| Per-order fee | −$0.40 |
| **You keep** | **$85.25** |

And in Books & Magazines, domestic: $100 − $15.30 − $0.40 = **$84.30**.

## Free Listings by Store Tier

eBay's zero insertion fee allowances scale with your Store subscription:

| Store tier | Zero insertion fee listings |
| --- | --- |
| Starter | 250 |
| Basic | 250 auction-style + 1,000 fixed price |
| Premium | 500 auction-style + 10,000 fixed price |
| Anchor | 1,000 auction-style + 25,000 fixed price |
| Enterprise | 2,500 auction-style + 100,000 fixed price |

:::tip
Store tiers cost a monthly subscription, so upgrading is the same arithmetic as any other platform decision: the higher tier pays for itself only if the extra listings and lower rates exceed the subscription cost at your actual volume. Work it out at the volume you have, not the one you're aiming for.
:::

## What This Means Before You Source Inventory

**Check the category rate before you buy stock.** A 15.3% category needs meaningfully better buying margins than a 6.7% one to produce the same profit.

**Remember shipping is inside the fee base.** Charging $10 shipping on a $40 item means the fee is calculated on $50, not $40.

**Budget the per-order fee on cheap items.** At $0.30–$0.40 it's negligible on a $200 sale and material on a $5 one — the same fixed-fee problem that makes low-priced digital products uneconomic elsewhere.

**Account for tax.** Reselling income is self-employment income; in the US, self-employment tax alone is 15.3% of net earnings before income tax.

:::action
Before your next sourcing run, look up the exact final value fee for the category you're buying into, then recompute your target buy price. Sellers who use one blanket "13%" assumption systematically overpay for stock in high-fee categories and leave margin on the table in low-fee ones.
:::
